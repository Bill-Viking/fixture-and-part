## Important
- Colour law is the visual thesis, not decoration: frozen = machinery (weights/dies), moving = the moving part (activations/tokens), keys = searchable metadata — don't swap this mapping. The tokens are named for those roles, so the colours filling them can be repainted (they have been, once) without touching the law.
- Zero layout shift is a standing invariant, and it is measured rather than assumed — CLS via a buffered PerformanceObserver, plus a geometry diff before and after the model load, because a shift below the fold does not register as CLS. The diff is over the page's structural boxes — the header, every section, every instrument slot and the foot — and not over every element in the document, so it catches a block that moves and would not catch a word that shifts inside one. Two shifts are known and accepted rather than fixed:
  - Instrument A's token strip reflows when the real model lands and the word-split pills are replaced by BPE pieces (~0.058 at 1280, and only while instrument A is on screen). The pills are genuinely different words of genuinely different widths; the strip's own height is already reserved, so nothing outside it moves. Inherent, pre-existing, and measured identically on the palette before this one.
  - The cache counter in instrument B reports ~2e-6 on the STEP that takes the entry count from one digit to two. The number's box is reserved and does not move; the digits inside it do.
  - Typing measures a hairline — 3.1e-5 at 1280, 6.8e-5 at 390 — from one text node re-wrapping in the prose that quotes the reader's sentence. Measured identically on main to the last digit, so it predates instrument F and the waterfall; it is a sub-pixel reflow of a curly quote, not anything moving.
- The walls are drawn from signed i8. `attn.c_attn.weight` is stored i8 at zero point 0, so a byte above 127 is a negative weight and 255 is −1; read unsigned, a wall paints the sign bit and every window's byte histogram is bimodal at the extremes, which is what the first build did. Brightness is the weight's magnitude — its size, not its sign, because both signs are the machinery working — and the middle-96% stretch is measured on the magnitudes, where it does real work: 0→33 of a largest 47 on block 0 down to 0→13 of 19 on block 5. Anything that reads those bytes again should read them signed.
- Perf framing, from the independent verification: like-for-like first paint favours this branch (116–124 ms against main's 216 ms); the FCP delta is +32 ms. The "332 vs 216" pairing in the production builder's own report compared two different things and should not be quoted.
  - Instrument F adds no third one, before the repaint or after it. On `memory-room`, model load, RUN THE PASS, a token click either way, BLOCK and HEAD chips, a wall click, OPEN IN THE FILE, STEP×2, the decoding toggle and the mode toggle all measure 0.000000 at both 1280 and 390, and the structural-box geometry diff across the model load moves nothing inside instrument F at either width (every mover is another instrument's own content: A's pills, B's candidate rows, C's query tokens, D's lens bars). The observer is proved rather than trusted — a 40 px block inserted into the instrument body in view reads 0.0128 at 1280 and 0.0252 at 390. Re-measured after the fix pass, with the plates and the register names moved above the fall: still 0.000000 for the model load, every token click, both chip rows, a wall click either way, RUN THE PASS and a token click at 21 tokens, at both widths, positive control 0.0143 / 0.0325. The one non-zero is the pre-existing prose reflow and it is identical on the branch before the fixes and after: one keystroke measures 0.000031 at 1280 and 0.000087 at 390, and it comes from `p.inst-reading`, the sentence quoted above the drawing, not from anything in the figure. Two shifts the old drawing had were fixed rather than accepted — a control swapping for a ghost line and moving the button beside it, and a right-aligned status note whose glyphs walked sideways whenever the wording changed length. Both are the same failure and have the same fix: pin the box, align the text left. Re-measured again after the full-bleed pass (2026-08-25), with the same harness run against the branch before it as a control: model load, three token clicks, both chip rows, a wall click either way, RUN THE PASS, STEP twice and a token click at 21 tokens are all still 0.000000 at both widths, the geometry diff across the model load still moves nothing inside F at either width (viewBox identical across the load), and 390 is identical to the control on every line including its positive control (0.0325). Two numbers at 1280 differ from the control and neither is a new shift. The prose hairline is now scored over a wider box, because the reading line it comes from is 1,166 px rather than 678: the same sub-pixel reflow reads 0.000114 for a keystroke where the control reads 0.000066, and 0.004888 against 0.002842 for replacing the whole sentence — one source, its box unchanged (`301,126,678,18` on the control and `57,126,1166,18` here, from == to in both), and the ratio is exactly 1166/678. CLS is area-weighted, so any element that gets wider scores the same movement higher; that is the cost of the ruling and not a regression in what moves. The other is the decoding toggle in instrument B, which reads 0.000695 when B itself is parked in view — byte-identical on the branch before this change, so it is B's own and predates the breakout. The earlier 0.000000 for it was measured with F parked, where B's shift sits off screen and is not reported.
- Instrument F is drawn in one law with three settings of it, and what a setting really chooses is how many pixels sit under a drawing unit. The drawing is 1,166 units wide at every width; the phone (≤ 640 px) renders it at about 342 px, so a unit is 0.29 px and the type is set roughly twice the column's; the column (641–1199 px) renders it at 678, 0.58 px a unit; the sheet (≥ 1200 px) renders it at 1,166, one pixel a unit, which is what comp-a3 was drawn at and where the type and the grain go back to the comp's own sizes. The stylesheet's breakout query and `FULL_MQ` in ForwardMap.jsx are the same 1,200 px and have to be changed together — if they ever disagree the drawing is laid out at the wrong scale. The rendered type barely moves across that boundary (a legend line is 9.6 px at 1199 and 10.1 px at 1200), which is the point: the drawing nearly doubles and the words stay the size they were.
- The height of instrument F's drawing is a function of its breakpoint and of nothing else — not of how many tokens are in the sequence, not of which register is open, not of whether a model has loaded. Measured across 0, 1, 8 and 21 tokens in both modes, with a register open and with an early hero: the viewBox is 1166 × 2238.75 at 1280 (rendered 1166 × 2238.75 px — a unit is a pixel there), 1166 × 2926.725 in the column setting between 641 and 1199 px, and 1166 × 4919 at 390 (rendered 320 × 1349.97). Two things pay for that. The legend prints live numbers, so each of its four entries has a reserved line count and clips rather than reflows (measured usage against the reservation: 6/7, 6/7, 8/9, 5/6 at 1280 and 7/8, 9/9, 13/14, 6/7 at 390 across illustrative, real, a long sentence and a two-token one; the column setting's was 8/9, 9/10, 12/13, 7/8). And the number of filaments a stream is drawn with steps with the token count — 64 × 12 dimensions up to twelve tokens, 32 × 24 to twenty-four, 16 × 48 beyond — which is stated on the drawing and also holds the element count roughly flat.
- Phase 1's numbers are explicitly heuristic, not real model output — keep that labelling intact if Phase 2 stays unmerged, so the two modes are never confused.
- Text drawn over a texture cannot be checked from the stylesheet. Instrument F's steel boxes carry a tensor thumbnail under their words, so the ground beneath a glyph is whatever that tensor's bytes happen to be there; contrast has to be measured off the raster (render twice, once with the text hidden, and score every glyph pixel against the pixel underneath it). Doing that found what a stylesheet sweep could not: the selected box's name line had dropped to 4.41:1, below AA across 99.4% of the glyph body. A naive `rgb()` regex is the other half of the same trap — anything built with `color-mix()` resolves to `color(srgb …)` and is skipped silently.
- The raster contrast method needs one more correction than it looks: a diff of the two renders finds every pixel the text touched, but most of those are the anti-aliased fringe, which is a blend of ink and ground by construction and scores near 1:1 whatever the design does. Solve for the coverage instead — the fill composited at the element's own opacity over the very ground under that pixel — and score only the pixels the glyph body owns. On the memory room that turns a meaningless 5.34% "below AA" into a real 0.14% (172 glyph pixels of 119,081 at 1280 real; 0.21% at 390; 0.04% at 1280 illustrative). It also found four real faults the stylesheet could not see, all of them low-opacity blue labels sitting on lit byte cells: the HEAD line at 3.73:1, the tensor name at 3.92:1, the window spec at 2.37:1 and the silent-register line at 1.51:1 median. Fixed by raising three opacities and by scrimming the wall behind a label in the screen's own ground. The threshold that word "body" means is α ≥ 0.8, and it has to be said out loud: the same frames scored at α ≥ 0.6 pull in the fringe band, where solving for the ink divides by a small coverage and the answer is mostly quantisation noise — the branch before the full-bleed pass reads 0.391% "below AA" that way and 0.000% at 0.8, on identical pixels. Re-measured at the full-bleed type sizes (2026-08-25, real mode, default sentence): 0.000% below AA at 1280 over 36,391 glyph-body pixels and 0.000% at 390 over 14,146, against 0.000% over 34,786 for the control at 1280, with every class's floor within a tenth of the control's (worst row `mr-tensor is-fine`, 4.79 → 4.75; the green key line 5.13 → 5.07).
- Instrument F draws no text over the picture without standing the picture back behind it. The wall dims to 55% under a stream and to 8% under a carrier, a label or a plate — and the dimming is a scrim painted in the screen's own background gradient rather than a per-cell alpha, which keeps all 9,216 wall cells out of every re-render. The scrim is close to what a per-cell alpha would compose to but not identical, and it is not claimed to be: the gradient is painted in the drawing's own coordinates, so a scrim is the ground at its own y, and what a cell keeps under it is 8% of a cell that is itself sitting on that ground. Measured off the raster, the ground under a scrimmed label reads 6–12 out of 255 per channel — the near-black of the screen — where an unscrimmed one read a lit byte cell. The other half of the rule is paint order: the words that name the drawing's parts are drawn after the fall, not before it, and each stands on a scrim sized from its own measured line width. The walls are memoised on the geometry alone: a keystroke redraws the streams, the carriers and the landing, and does not touch a single byte cell.
- Instrument F's lens whisper was removed in the arc 4 repaint. The drawing now says what the machine settled on at the landing instead, and instrument D still presents the same seven depths as a table, which is where they can actually be read. `src/lib/tensorTexture.js` and the `thumbnails` block of `fileFacts.json` are left in place but are no longer drawn by anything — dead weight to clear when something else touches that file. The thumbnails are ~70.8 kB, 32% of the shipped `fileFacts` chunk, and they still ship: clearing them means regenerating `fileFacts.json`, and the file is deliberately byte-identical to main for the whole of arc 4 so that the walls can be proved to be the same bytes the deployed page already reads. Deferred on purpose, not overlooked.
