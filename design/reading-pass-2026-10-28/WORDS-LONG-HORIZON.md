## P1 (essay paragraph)

The dial that does move is how far a rule carries. Loss falls smoothly with the size of the file and the amount of text it was trained on — a curve, not a step — and the length of task a model can finish at a fixed success rate has grown with it: measured on software work, that horizon has doubled about every seven months since 2019. So a rule is lost at a rate, not at a wall. A smaller model holds the same rulebook in its context and lets it slip from the spotlight a few steps sooner, the way anything placed in the middle of a long context is used less than what sits at either end.

## P2 (essay paragraph)

One morning’s count, from this page’s own harness, not a measurement: the same written rules were in front of two models of one family. The smaller needed four replies to hand over one package that the larger handed over in one, and nothing in the rules had changed between them — only how long a rule stayed in the spotlight while the steps piled up. That is the shape of the rate. It is also why the harness in the specimen above and a bigger file are two different roads to a long horizon: the harness keeps the rule on the page at every step; the bigger file keeps it in the spotlight longer on its own.
