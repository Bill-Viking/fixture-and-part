# WORDS — the two modes, spelled out (drafted 2026-10-28 21:5x by the lane; Bill: «we need to spell this out clearly and in detail»; his to strike)

Where: a callout in section 01, directly above instrument E's mode bar — the first place a reader meets the mode chips — in BOTH copies; the company copy's variant drops the first paragraph's «press the button» clause. Label: `THE TWO MODES — WHAT IS COMPUTING`.

Every instrument on this page runs in one of two modes, and the chips on its bar say which.

REAL. The actual model file, 83.5 MB of frozen weights, runs on your sentence inside your browser. Every number an instrument shows is what that network produced: which earlier words it attended to and by how much, how each word’s vector grew as it fell through the six blocks, which word it predicts next. Change the sentence and the model runs again. The numbers mean something about language, because the weights were learned from text. (Public copy: press the load button once; the file stays in your browser after.)

ILLUSTRATIVE. No model runs. A stand-in makes plausible-looking numbers from your words: the same word always gets the same made-up vector, the attention table follows a rule of thumb about nearby words, the predictions are hand-set. Think of a mannequin in the shape of a person: it has a head, arms and legs where the instruments need them, so the layout works and you can learn what each panel is for — but it knows nothing. Change the sentence and the mannequin changes pose; it is never stale, and it was never telling you anything true about the sentence.

What that means for you: in illustrative mode you can learn how to read the instruments; you cannot learn anything about your sentence from them. The two things on this page that are real in both modes are the file’s own bytes (the walls of instrument F, the readings of instrument E), read from the model file when the page was built — and, in the company copy, one real pass of the page’s own sentence, kept and replayed in instrument F.
