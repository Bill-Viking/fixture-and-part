## A. David Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature*, Book 1, Part 4, Section 6, "Of personal identity"

- url used: https://davidhume.org/texts/t/1/4/6 — **LOADED (free full text)**
- Fallback (Gutenberg #4705) was not needed.
- Page title, verbatim (as rendered on the page): **"SECT. VI. Of personal identity."**

Verbatim quotes, with the site's own paragraph numbers:

- **"when I enter most intimately..." passage (¶ T 1.4.6.3):**
  > "For my part, when I enter most intimately into what I call myself, I always stumble on some particular perception or other, of heat or cold, light or shade, love or hatred, pain or pleasure. I never can catch myself at any time without a perception, and never can observe any thing but the perception."

- **"bundle or collection of different perceptions" sentence (¶ T 1.4.6.4):**
  > "they are nothing but a bundle or collection of different perceptions, which succeed each other with an inconceivable rapidity, and are in a perpetual flux and movement."

- **Theatre passage (¶ T 1.4.6.4, same paragraph as the bundle sentence):**
  > "The mind is a kind of theatre, where several perceptions successively make their appearance; pass, re-pass, glide away, and mingle in an infinite variety of postures and situations."

All three are usable verbatim for the THEORY 1 note (Hume's bundle view — "found only the parade").

## B. The Buddhist not-self teaching — Anattalakkhana Sutta (SN 22.59)

- url used: https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn22/sn22.059.than.html (Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation) — **LOADED (free full text)**
- Page title as displayed: **"Pañcavaggi Sutta: Five Brethren"**, with the subtitle line **"(aka: Anatta-lakkhana Sutta: The Discourse on the Not-self Characteristic)"** — so this is in fact SN 22.59 under Thanissaro's chosen title; the "Anattalakkhana Sutta" name is given as the alternate/traditional title on the same page.

Verbatim quotes:

- **Opening declarative pattern** (repeated once per aggregate — form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness):
  > "Form, monks, is not self. If form were the self, this form would not lend itself to dis-ease..."
  > (and, for the fifth aggregate) "Consciousness is not self. If consciousness were the self, this consciousness would not lend itself to dis-ease..."

- **Question-and-answer pattern that follows, for each aggregate** (this carries the exact "not mine / not I am / not my self" wording the essay note wants):
  > "What do you think, monks — Is form constant or inconstant?" / "Inconstant, lord." / "And is that which is inconstant easeful or stressful?" / "Stressful, lord." / "And is it fitting to regard what is inconstant, stressful, subject to change as: 'This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am'?" / "No, lord."

- **Concluding "seen as it actually is" instruction, per aggregate:**
  > "Any form whatsoever that is past, future, or present; internal or external; blatant or subtle; common or sublime; far or near: every form is to be seen as it actually is with right discernment as: 'This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am.'"

Note for the note-writer: the page's own rendering does not use the exact string "this is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self" (that phrasing is closer to Ñanamoli's or Bodhi's translation); Thanissaro's page instead reads **"This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am."** If the essay wants the "this I am not" wording specifically, that is a different translator (suttacentral.net's Bodhi-derived rendering) — not yet fetched. Flag before quoting if the exact phrasing matters.

## D. Windt, J. M., Nielsen, T. & Thompson, E. (2016), "Does consciousness disappear in dreamless sleep?", *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 20(12), 871–882

- url used: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27765420/ — **WALL** (cookie-consent shell only; also see PMID correction below)
- url used: https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi + efetch.fcgi (NCBI E-utilities) — **LOADED** — used to find/confirm the correct PMID (see note)
- url used: https://evanthompson.me/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/1-s2-0-s1364661316301528-main.pdf (Evan Thompson's own author site — one of the paper's three authors) — **LOADED (free full text, the actual publisher-typeset PDF, first 3 pages read in full)**

**PMID correction:** the brief's PMID, 27765420, resolves on PubMed/NCBI to a *different, unrelated* article — "Simultaneous quantification of propranolol and sulfamethoxazole and major human metabolite conjugates... in municipal wastewater" (Brown & Wong, *J Chromatogr A* 2016;1471:34-44), confirmed via both efetch and esummary. The correct PMID for Windt, Nielsen & Thompson 2016, found by searching NCBI for the DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2016.09.006`, is **27765517**. Confirmed via efetch: Title "Does Consciousness Disappear in Dreamless Sleep?", Trends Cogn Sci. 2016 Dec;20(12):871-882, authors Windt JM, Nielsen T, Thompson E — this matches the DOI, journal, volume/issue/pages given in the brief exactly; only the PMID digit-string was wrong (looks like a transposition: ...420 vs. the correct ...517).

Verbatim abstract (from the free full-text PDF, page 871):
> "Consciousness is often said to disappear in deep, dreamless sleep. We argue that this assumption is oversimplified. Unless dreamless sleep is defined as unconscious from the outset there are good empirical and theoretical reasons for saying that a range of different types of sleep experience, some of which are distinct from dreaming, can occur in all stages of sleep. We introduce a novel taxonomy for describing different kinds of dreamless sleep experiences and suggest research methods for their investigation. Future studies should focus on three areas: memory consolidation, sleep disorders, and sleep state (mis)perception. Our proposal suggests new directions for sleep and dream science, as well as for the neuroscience of consciousness, and can also inform the diagnosis and treatment of sleep disorders."

Verbatim thesis statement (body text, page 871, under "Challenging the Default View of Consciousness in Sleep"):
> "We argue here that, contrary to the default view, dreamless sleep cannot accurately be described as a uniform state of unconsciousness. Instead, sleep supports a range of different types of experience that are distinct from dreaming and occur outside rapid eye movement (REM) sleep."

Verbatim hedge sentence (page 872, important for the note's "is argued" framing — the authors are NOT claiming certainty):
> "Notably, this is not to claim that conscious mental activity continues throughout sleep. Whether periods of unconsciousness occur in sleep alongside dreaming and dreamless sleep experience is, in our view, an open empirical question. The more pressing point is that, given the available evidence, it seems unlikely that all stages of sleep currently classed as dreamless necessarily or consistently involve a loss of phenomenal consciousness."

This fully supports the note's planned framing: "whether dreamless sleep is truly without experience is argued" — the paper's own abstract calls the default (blank/no-experience) view "oversimplified" and its body explicitly declines to claim certainty either way, calling the underlying question "open."
